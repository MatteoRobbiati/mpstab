QPU¶

Everything needed to run an HQSMPO head/tail split on a quantum device. The pipeline is three steps, one subpackage each, and every circuit is decided classically before anything reaches a backend.

Running part of an expectation value on a QPU.

HQSMPO splits a circuit’s dressed rotations into a head, run on the device, and a tail, folded classically into the observable. Getting from that split to a number is a three-step pipeline, one subpackage each:

  1. synthesis turns the head into a runnable circuit plus a pure-Clifford residual, folded into the observable.

  2. measurement fixes the circuits and shots that measure the folded observable, and turns the measured frequencies into an ExpectationResult.

  3. execution runs those circuits on a backend, or exports them as OpenQASM to run on a device.

Synthesisers and measurement methods are objects holding their own options, passed to HQSMPO:

from mpstab import HQSMPO
from mpstab.qpu import ClassicalShadows, ExtractionSynthesis

model = HQSMPO(ansatz, head_synthesis=ExtractionSynthesis(connectivity="line"))
result = model.expectation_at_cut(H, cut_index, method=ClassicalShadows(), n_shots=10_000)

Importing this package never requires the optional rustiq dependency: rustiq only needs it when it synthesises.

Synthesis¶

Turns a head’s dressed rotations into a runnable circuit plus a pure-Clifford residual.

Step 1 of the QPU pipeline: head synthesis.

A head of dressed Pauli rotations is turned into a hardware-native gate list plus a pure-Clifford residual applied last:

target = U(residual) . U(head)
<target|O|target> = <head 0| residual^dag . O . residual |head 0>

The residual is therefore reabsorbed into the observable with a Clifford simulator (conjugate()), never with dense matrices.

A synthesiser is a HeadSynthesis holding its own options. Three are available, one module each:

  • RustiqSynthesis: rustiq’s Pauli-network synthesis (optional dependency, needed only when synthesising).

  • ExtractionSynthesis: Clifford extraction, dependency-free and connectivity-aware.

  • CnotLadderSynthesis: the textbook decomposition, with an identity residual.

synthesize(paulis, angles) returns (head, residual_tableau, residual_gates), with head in the gate-list format of utils.

Head synthesis by Clifford extraction (the idea of QuCLEAR, arXiv:2408.13316).

Each rotation is reduced to a single qubit by a Clifford that is never undone but pushed through the rotations still to come. It needs no extra dependency and can be told the device connectivity, so that the CNOTs it chooses stay local.

Bases: HeadSynthesis

Synthesise a head circuit and its pure-Clifford residual by Clifford extraction. Deterministic.

A rotation of a weight-w string needs w - 1 CNOTs to bring it onto one qubit, and the textbook circuit spends as many again to undo them. Here they are not undone: the Clifford is pushed through the rotations still to come, whose strings change accordingly, and what has accumulated at the end is the residual, folded into the observable. This is the idea of QuCLEAR (Liu et al., arXiv:2408.13316); the choice of each CNOT is our own greedy rule, see _extraction_skeleton().

Every strategy of strategies is run and the cheapest head kept, cost being the two-qubit gates the device would need: with a connectivity a CNOT between distant qubits is charged the SWAPs that route it.

Parameters:

  • connectivity – None (all-to-all), "line", or the edges [(q0, q1), ...] of the device coupling graph, in the qubit numbering of the strings. The CNOTs chosen stay local on it.

  • strategies – the settings to try, keeping the cheapest head, see EXTRACTION_STRATEGIES.

class mpstab.qpu.synthesis.cnot_ladder.CnotLadderSynthesis[source]¶

Bases: HeadSynthesis

Decompose every dressed rotation into an exact CNOT-ladder circuit.

Uses the textbook identity exp(-i theta P / 2) = U^dag L^dag RZ(theta) L U, where U rotates every non-identity site of P into the Z frame and L is a CNOT ladder chaining the parity of the support onto its last qubit. Every gate is placed exactly, so nothing is left over and the residual is the identity – at the cost of 2*(w-1) CNOTs per weight-w rotation and no cancellation across rotations.

Measurement¶

Fixes the circuits and shots that measure the tail-folded observable, and recombines the measured frequencies into an expectation value with its error bars. One module per method.

Step 2 of the QPU pipeline: measuring the tail-folded observable.

A method is a MeasurementMethod holding its own options, with both halves of the job: building the MeasurementPlan (circuits and shots, fixed before anything runs) and estimating an ExpectationResult from the measured frequencies. One module each:

  • PauliSampling ("pauli"): sampled Pauli strings, grouped into qubit-wise-commuting settings.

  • ClassicalShadows ("shadows"): random Pauli bases, contracted with the observable MPO.

pauli_expansion turns the observable MPO into Pauli strings and variances, for the methods above.

What every measurement method shares: the plan it builds before anything runs, and the result it estimates afterwards.

A MeasurementPlan fixes every circuit and its shot count up front, so the same plan can be replayed on a simulator or on hardware, and estimation only ever reads back frequencies.

Basis rotation is left to qibo: gates.M(*qubits, basis=[...]) measures each qubit in the given single-qubit Pauli basis, so no basis-change circuit is built here.

property total_error: float¶

A practical accuracy bound, not a standard error itself: sqrt(stderr**2 + truncation_l2**2 + bond_truncation_l2**2) (the last term for "pauli" only, where truncation_l2 is the Pauli-set truncation and the bond truncation is reported apart), combining contributions of different nature in quadrature. stderr is a statistical quantity (shot noise, shrinks with more shots); truncation_l2 is a systematic-bias estimate (bond-dimension or Pauli-coverage truncation, independent of shot count – see truncation_l1 and retained_weight to tell which one is dominating). Combining them in quadrature is a convenient, common convention, not a rigorous derivation of a single confidence interval – but it is still the number to report in a results table, since stderr alone omits the truncation bias entirely. For "simulation" it covers the tail truncation only; the head truncation shows in state_fidelity.

property group_variances: tuple¶

Per-group a-priori variance, sum_{P in group} |c_P|**2.

Each member’s single-shot parity variance is bounded by 1, saturated when <P> = 0, and covariance between members of a group is neglected. Needs no sampling and no state, which is what lets shot allocation and shot budgeting be decided up front.

shots_for_precision(epsilon: float) → float[source]¶

Total shots predicted for standard error epsilon under variance-proportional (Neyman-optimal) allocation across groups, (sum_G sqrt(V_G))**2 / epsilon**2.

By Cauchy-Schwarz sqrt(V_G) <= sum_{P in G} |c_P|, so this is never above the ungrouped ||c||_1**2 / epsilon**2: the difference is what grouping saves.

mpstab.qpu.measurement.pauli.allocate_shots_by_variance(plan: PauliMeasurementPlan, n_shots: int) → tuple[source]¶

Split n_shots across groups proportionally to sqrt(V_G), respecting n_shots exactly.

This is the Neyman-optimal allocation, and matches the variance model PauliMeasurementPlan.shots_for_precision() sizes the total against. Every group needs at least one shot to be measured at all, so one shot is reserved for each group first; the remainder is allocated by weight and rounded with the largest-remainder method, so the returned shot counts always sum to exactly n_shots (rounding a raw fractional allocation per group, as a naive round() would, can drift the total away from n_shots in either direction).

Raises:

ValueError – if n_shots is smaller than the number of groups, since every group needs at least one shot.

mpstab.qpu.measurement.pauli.group_qwc(strings, weights=None) → tuple[source]¶

Group Pauli strings into qubit-wise-commuting sets, largest coefficient first.

Only qubit-wise commutation is exploited, not the more general “fully commuting” partition, because the latter needs an entangling Clifford basis change that would add two-qubit gates to the very head circuit this scheme exists to keep cheap. Minimising the number of QWC groups is NP-hard, so this greedy count is an upper bound on the true number of settings.

Parameters:

  • strings – Pauli strings to group.

  • weights – optional per-string sort key, e.g. |c_P|.

Returns:

A tuple of QWCGroup, with leftover I in each merged setting resolved to Z.

The "shadows" method: classical shadows from random single-qubit Pauli bases, contracted with the tail-folded observable MPO through the canonical dual.

Random local Pauli-basis measurements are the six-effect-per-qubit POVM

Pi_k in {|0><0|/3, |1><1|/3, |+><+|/3, |-><-|/3, |+i><+i|/3, |-i><-i|/3}

indexed k = 0..5 in that order (outcome_index()). Every operator is handled as the MPS of its Pauli coefficients (O = sum_P c_P P, as mpo_to_pauli_mps() returns it). Because each effect is a fixed combination of single-qubit Paulis, the canonical dual is a site-local map (LOCAL_CANONICAL_DUAL_MAP): applied to O’s Pauli-coefficient MPS it gives, at O’s own bond dimension, the MPS omega of per-outcome estimator values (canonical_omega_seed()). Estimating is then a batched lookup of omega at every measured outcome (evaluate_omega()).

mpstab.qpu.measurement.shadows.real_pauli_mps(pauli_mps: list) → list[source]¶

An exactly equivalent, real-valued copy of a Pauli-coefficient MPS.

A Hermitian operator’s Pauli coefficients are real, but the MPS mpo_to_pauli_mps() returns can still carry complex gauge phases on its bonds – and the real part of a product is not the product of the real parts, so taking site.real site by site would describe a different operator. Instead every site A = R + iI becomes the real block [[R, -I], [I, R]] (the 2x2 real representation of a complex number, bond dimension doubled), and the boundary sites keep only the row/column that selects the real part of the full contraction. The doubled train is then recompressed exactly by _compress_real_mps(): the coefficients being real, the rank of every unfolding is the same over the reals as over the complexes, so the bond dimension comes back to at most the original one. Sites that are already real are returned as they are, at their own bond dimension.

Pauli-basis expansion of an MPO: exact coefficients, sampling and variances.

The tail of a head/tail split is a Pauli rotation chain folded into the observable, which leaves an MPO. To measure that observable on a device it has to be turned back into Pauli strings with coefficients. This module does that change of basis and everything that follows directly from it.

Every function takes the MPO as its site arrays, the engine-independent form operator_site_arrays() exports: one numpy array per site ordered (left, right, k, b), with k the upper (output) and b the lower (input) index, and boundary bonds as singleton axes. The recurring contraction is Tr[sigma O] = sum_{k,b} O[k,b] sigma[b,k], hence the "kb,bk" index patterns below.

mpstab.qpu.measurement.pauli_expansion.fold_pool_through_tableau(pool: dict, residual_tableau) → dict[source]¶

Conjugate every pooled {pauli: coefficient} entry through residual_tableau.

A device running a resynthesised head prepares U(head)|psi_0>, not the exact head state the tail-folded coefficients were derived against; the two differ by the resynthesis Clifford residual. Every sampled string therefore has to be conjugated through that residual before QWC grouping, since conjugation does not preserve qubit-wise commutativity and folding afterwards would silently invalidate the measurement settings. Strings that collide after folding are summed.

Parameters:

  • residual_tableau – the Clifford residual R, a stim.Tableau; every string P becomes R^dag P R.

mpstab.qpu.measurement.pauli_expansion.mpo_to_pauli_mps(mpo) → list[source]¶

Rewrite an MPO in the Pauli basis, exactly.

Each site tensor A[l, r, k, b] becomes B[i, l, r] with physical dimension 4, one entry per label of PAULI_LABELS, and unchanged bond dimension. Contracting the resulting train at a fixed label sequence P gives c_P = Tr[O P] / 2**n. Being only a change of basis on the physical index, it makes sample_pauli_strings() exact and shadow_variance_from_mpo() enumeration-free.

Exact single-snapshot classical-shadow variance of mpo, sum_P |c_P|**2 * 3**w_P.

mpstab.qpu.measurement.pauli_expansion.truncation_error_estimate(ensemble: PauliEnsemble)[source]¶

Estimate the systematic error from the Pauli strings ensemble left out.

The discarded operator contributes at most sum |c_P| (L1) and sqrt(sum |c_P|**2) (L2) over the discarded set, if that set could be summed exactly. The L2 value returned is exact – it comes straight from what PauliEnsemble already carries (the total weight minus the retained weight). The L1 value is not a rigorous bound: the discarded set is in general exponentially large and never enumerated, so it is only a heuristic extrapolation from the sampled tail – the smallest retained |c_P| sets an assumed coefficient scale at the sampling threshold, and dividing the exact discarded L2 mass by that scale gives an effective discarded-term count whose L1 mass is discarded_mass / c_min. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude indicator, not a certified error bound.

Returns:

(l1_estimate, l2_exact).

Execution¶

Runs a plan’s circuits on a backend, or exports them as OpenQASM to run on a device.

Step 3 of the QPU pipeline: running a plan’s circuits, here or elsewhere.

  • simulator: the backend contract and the default QiboSimulator.

  • qasm: OpenQASM 2 export of a plan’s circuits.

  • storage: saving them to a folder, so they can run on a device.

Running a MeasurementPlan on a backend.

A backend is any object with an execute_circuits(circuits, nshots) method returning one frequency dictionary per circuit, as qibo’s Circuit.__call__(nshots=...).frequencies() does; there is no base class to subclass. QiboSimulator below is the default.

Bitstrings are qubit-0-leftmost throughout mpstab, as in qibo. A backend whose provider reports them the other way round, qubit 0 rightmost as in qiskit, must reverse them before returning.

class mpstab.qpu.execution.simulator.QiboSimulator[source]¶

Bases: object

Runs circuits with qibo’s own simulator. The default backend.

Measurement plans run one state preparation under many measurement bases, so circuits are grouped by their preparation, everything before the basis rotations of their final gates.M: each preparation is simulated once, and each circuit then only rotates that state into its own basis and samples it. Every circuit still gets its own nshots independent samples, so the results are distributed exactly as if every circuit had been run separately. Circuits of any other shape (density matrices, noise channels, several or mid-circuit measurements) are simulated as they are, identical ones once.

mpstab.qpu.execution.simulator.execute_plan(backend, plan) → list[source]¶

Run every circuit in a MeasurementPlan with its own shot count, batching circuits that share a shot count into a single backend.execute_circuits call instead of one call per circuit.

A "shadows" plan’s circuits mostly share shots_per_setting shots each, so this collapses what would otherwise be one backend call per shot into a handful of calls – the contract already supports a batch (execute_circuits takes a list), the naive call site just wasn’t using it.

Parameters:

  • backend – anything with execute_circuits(circuits, nshots).

  • plan – a plan whose circuits and shots are the same length.

Returns:

One frequency dict per circuit, in plan.circuits order and qubit-0-leftmost – what measurement assumes.

Raises:

ValueError – if the backend returns a different number of results than the circuits it was given, which would otherwise leave gaps that only surface later, inside the estimator.

OpenQASM 2 export of a plan’s circuits, the form a device backend works on; save_circuits() writes them to disk for an offline run.

mpstab.qpu.execution.qasm.circuit_to_qasm(circuit) → str[source]¶

OpenQASM 2 text of a qibo circuit, e.g. one of a plan’s circuits.

Only the gates of the original qelib1.inc (QELIB1_GATES) are written by name, so any OpenQASM 2 parser reads the text without custom definitions. Every other one-qubit gate becomes a u3 gate equal to it up to a global phase: the Unitary basis changes a plan adds before measurement, which qibo’s own Circuit.to_qasm() rejects, and gates such as SX/SXDG that rustiq’s resynthesis emits, which qiskit’s parser rejects by default. Qubit i is measured into c[i], so a provider reporting counts as c[n-1]...c[0] (as qiskit does) returns them qubit 0 rightmost: reverse them before estimating.

This only translates the circuit: mapping it to a device’s native gates and qubits is the provider’s transpiler’s job.

Raises:

ValueError – on a gate acting on more than one qubit that is not in QELIB1_GATES.
